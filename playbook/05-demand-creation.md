# 5. Demand creation

## Why it matters

Most of your future buyers are not searching yet. Demand creation teaches the right accounts who you are before they need you, so they search for you, take the meeting and close faster later. Capture runs out without it.

## Ask first

- Who exactly are you trying to reach: which accounts, which titles? Is the target account list agreed with Sales? ([02-align-to-the-business.md](02-align-to-the-business.md))
- What do you have to say that a buyer would stop for: a point of view, data, a useful tool? Ads amplify a message; they can't invent one.
- Do you ask "how did you hear about us" on every demo form, as an open text field?
- Can you leave this running for two quarters before judging it? If not, stay in capture for now.
- How much is creation getting? Default: 40% of budget ([defaults](../deliverables/defaults.md), [06-budget.md](06-budget.md)).

## Do this

1. **Judge creation on the right things.** Primary measures: reach and engagement among target accounts, pipeline velocity (how fast engaged accounts move stages), and self-reported "how did you hear about us". Use a longer window: a full sales cycle or two. Never judge it on last click.
2. **Prove cause with lift.** Split your target accounts into a test group and a holdout group that sees no ads. After a quarter, compare meetings, SAOs and branded search between them. Use platform lift studies where offered.
3. **Credit with honesty.** In the [budget tracker](../deliverables/budget-tracker.csv), count creation pipeline from self-reported source and influenced accounts, labelled lower confidence ([03-measurement.md](03-measurement.md)).
4. **Start with one channel done well:** LinkedIn for most B2B SaaS, then add one more once you can see lift.

### Paid social: LinkedIn, Meta, Reddit

1. **LinkedIn first.** Target by company list and job function or title. Run thought leadership and short video to the whole list; save lead forms for warm audiences.
2. **Meta** works when your buyers are reachable as people (small business owners, founders) or for cheap retargeting of site visitors. Test it with a capped budget.
3. **Reddit** suits technical and practitioner buyers who research in communities. Target subreddits and keywords; write like a member, not a brand.
4. Refresh creative every four to six weeks (rule of thumb) or when frequency climbs and engagement drops.
5. **Primary metric:** engagement rate and reach among target accounts; then meetings from engaged accounts.
6. **Best sources:** [LinkedIn Marketing Solutions blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog) and the [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/b2b-institute) for B2B brand research; [Reddit for Business Learning Hub](https://business.reddit.com/learning-hub).

### YouTube and connected TV

1. Use YouTube to put short explainer and customer-story video in front of custom segments (people who searched your category, or your site visitors).
2. CTV is a brand play for later. Wait until you have a strong video and enough budget to reach your markets repeatedly.
3. Measure with a brand lift study or a geo test, and watch branded search in the regions you ran.
4. **Primary metric:** cost per completed view among your audience, then lift in branded search.
5. **Best sources:** [YouTube Ads](https://www.youtube.com/ads/); [Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/) for video research.

### Display and retargeting

1. Retarget visitors of high-intent pages (pricing, demo, comparison) with proof: case studies, reviews, a clear next step.
2. Cap frequency and exclude customers and open opportunities.
3. Use one account-level placement exclusion list for junk apps and sites.
4. **Primary metric:** return visits and meetings from retargeted accounts, not view-through conversions.
5. **Best source:** [Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads) for audience and placement setup.

### ABM platforms

1. Buy an ABM platform only once Sales works an agreed target account list and you need account-level intent and reach. Before that, LinkedIn company lists do most of the job.
2. Tier accounts (1: one-to-one, 2: one-to-few, 3: one-to-many) and set ads, email and Sales plays per tier.
3. Feed account engagement to Sales weekly, with the next step for each hot account.
4. **Primary metric:** target accounts engaged, and stage movement versus a holdout group.
5. **Best sources:** [Demandbase](https://www.demandbase.com/) for ABM method; [Refine Labs blog](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog) for measuring B2B demand.

### Sponsorships: newsletters, podcasts, events

1. Pick by audience fit, not size. Ask for the subscriber or attendee breakdown by title and company size.
2. Buy a run of three or more placements (rule of thumb); one-offs are hard to read.
3. Give each sponsorship its own link and a named offer, and add it as a choice on "how did you hear about us".
4. At events, book meetings before you go. Count meetings held and SAOs, not badge scans.
5. **Primary metric:** self-reported mentions and meetings per dollar.
6. **Best sources:** [Exit Five](https://www.exitfive.com/) for B2B sponsorship and community; [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/blog/) for events and go-to-market.

### Content syndication

1. Syndication buys leads on your content from publisher lists. Treat them as cold: nurture, don't hand them straight to Sales.
2. Filter to your target accounts and titles in the contract. Pay only for leads that match.
3. **Primary metric:** share of leads that reach SQL within two quarters.
4. **Best source:** [Refine Labs blog](https://www.refinelabs.com/blog) for a skeptical view of gated lead volume.

### Email and lifecycle (alongside both)

1. Nurture everyone who shows interest, by role and stage. Send useful content, not "just checking in".
2. Send product-led signups an onboarding sequence that drives the activation step tied to conversion.
3. Suppress open opportunities and customers from acquisition email.
4. **Primary metric:** MQL to SQL rate from nurtured leads; activation rate for signups.

## If you see X, do Y

- **High CTR, no meetings after a quarter:** check that you reach target accounts, not random clickers. Tighten the company list.
- **Low engagement on LinkedIn:** change the message before the targeting. Try a sharper point of view or a person's voice.
- **Leadership asks for last-click ROI:** show the holdout test, self-reported source and branded search trend instead ([08-reporting-and-communication.md](08-reporting-and-communication.md)).
- **Branded search and direct traffic rise after a push:** creation is working. Record it ([11-cross-channel-and-working-with-others.md](11-cross-channel-and-working-with-others.md)).
- **Syndication leads never convert:** cut the vendor or move to pay-per-target-account.
- **Frequency high, results falling:** new creative or a wider list.

## Metrics

- Target accounts reached and engaged (by tier).
- Pipeline velocity: days between stages for engaged vs not-engaged accounts.
- Self-reported source mentions per month.
- Lift versus holdout accounts: meetings, SAOs, branded search.
- Influenced pipeline, labelled lower confidence.

## Common mistakes

- Judging creation on cost per lead or last-click pipeline, then cutting it.
- Gating everything behind forms and handing the leads to Sales.
- Running too many channels at once with too little budget each.
- No holdout, so nothing can be proven.
- Stopping after six weeks. Creation pays back over a sales cycle or more.

## Call an expert when

- You are choosing an ABM platform or signing a contract over one year.
- You need a lift test or holdout designed and read properly.
- You plan CTV or a large event budget.
